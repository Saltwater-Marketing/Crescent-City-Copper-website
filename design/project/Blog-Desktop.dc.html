How to Measure a Door or Window for a Copper Awning

Four measurements and two photos are all we need to size an awning. Here's where to put the tape.

By [Author name] · [Month DD, YYYY] · 5 min read

Most of the calls we get start with "I'm not sure what size I need." That's the right instinct: a standard size that's two inches too narrow looks wrong for as long as the copper lasts. Measure once with these steps, and we'll confirm the size with you before anything is cut.

1. Measure the width across the trim

Measure from the outside edge of the trim on one side to the outside edge on the other. If there's no trim, measure the opening and tell us so. Most awnings are sized to overhang the trim by [X in, TBC] on each side.

2. Measure the space above

Measure from the top of the trim to the nearest thing above it: a soffit, a gutter, a second-floor window sill. Our standard awnings are 24 or 30 inches high, so this number tells us which heights will fit.

3. Note what's on the wall

Brick, stucco, wood siding and fiber-cement each take different fasteners. Also note lights, house numbers or a doorbell near the top of the trim; the brackets have to land clear of them.

4. Take two photos

One straight-on photo of the whole door or window with the wall around it, and one close-up with the tape measure across the trim. Email both to info@crescentcitycopper.com with your measurements. They answer most of the questions we'd otherwise have to ask.

When to go custom

If your width falls between our standard sizes, or the space above is tight, we build to your numbers. Custom-built awnings can't be returned, which is why we confirm every measurement by phone before cutting copper.
